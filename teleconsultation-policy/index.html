Teleconsultation Policy

1. Scope and suitability

Teleconsultation may be offered for selected first assessments and follow-ups when the practitioner considers remote care clinically appropriate. It may not suit concerns requiring palpation, procedures, immediate examination, urgent investigation, or reliable physical observations. The practitioner may pause the consultation and recommend local in-person or emergency care.

Patients must be physically located in a jurisdiction where the practitioner may lawfully provide the service.

2. Consent, identity, and participation

By proceeding, you consent to remote communication and acknowledge its limitations. The clinic may verify the patient’s name, age, location, contact details, and identity. A parent or guardian must participate for a minor. You may withdraw from teleconsultation and request an in-person option, subject to availability and safety.

3. Preparing for the appointment

  • Use a private, well-lit space with a stable connection.
  • Have current medicines, prescriptions, reports, and symptom records available.
  • Join from a device that allows you to hear and respond clearly.
  • Provide your current location and a backup telephone number.
  • Do not drive or perform hazardous tasks during the consultation.

4. Privacy, recording, and technology

Reasonable efforts are made to use suitable communication channels, but remote systems carry risks including interception, device access, connection failure, and unintended disclosure. Patients should secure their own device and surroundings. Neither party may record, photograph, or distribute the consultation without explicit prior agreement and any consent required by law.

If the connection fails, the clinic may call the supplied number, reschedule, or recommend in-person care. Clinical notes are handled under the Privacy Policy.

5. Clinical limits and prescriptions

A remote consultation may provide assessment, education, homeopathic recommendations, monitoring, or referral, but cannot guarantee diagnosis or outcome. Any medicine or advice depends on information reasonably available. Tell the practitioner about allergies, pregnancy, other treatment, and changes in symptoms.

Do not stop or alter prescribed conventional medication without the prescribing clinician. Investigations, physical examination, or another clinician’s opinion may be required before or after teleconsultation.

6. Emergencies and urgent concerns

Teleconsultation is not an emergency service. For severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, loss of consciousness, uncontrolled bleeding, stroke signs, severe allergic reaction, suicidal crisis, rapidly worsening symptoms, or another emergency, contact local emergency services or attend the nearest emergency department immediately.

Do not send an email, form, or WhatsApp message and wait for a response.

7. Fees, cancellation, and follow-up

Applicable fees, payment timing, cancellation terms, and any courier costs are communicated before the appointment. A consultation may be rescheduled if technology prevents a clinically adequate interaction. Follow-up timing, reports, referrals, and any need for in-person review are explained during or after the visit.
